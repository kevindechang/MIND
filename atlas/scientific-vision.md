# Digital Mind — Scientific Vision
**Research Atlas v0.1, 2026-10-10 · Status: research program, not proven architecture.**

## Executive proposition

Can one artificial system exhibit persistent, causally integrated, developing functional cognition over its operational history, while remaining scientifically honest about what those accomplishments do—and do not—imply about conscious experience?

The engineering objective is **one enduring, developing digital entity**, not a collage of evocative language-model behaviors. This program is agnostic about whether its best computational substrate is an LLM with external state, recurrent neural computation, predictive-state control, neurosymbolic reasoning, artificial-life dynamics or an alternative. The user-facing exemplars Joi and Samantha are fictional *presence and development* inspirations; they are neither neuroscientific evidence nor requirements to simulate intimate dependence.

## Claims ladder and scientific limits

- **L1 — behavioral presence:** observers perceive coherent identity, memory, initiative, self-correction and social competence across sessions. This can be produced by purely functional mechanisms. Objective action outcomes are needed alongside blind impressions.
- **L2 — causal integrated cognition:** selective interventions on durable internal variables change future behavior beyond matched reactive, retrieval and compact-state controllers. Trace actual state and compute through interruptions. No amount of fluent introspection substitutes for ablation and prediction.
- **L3 — phenomenal consciousness:** disputed between theories and perhaps underdetermined even with extensive functional measurements. Neither a model's self-report nor L1/L2 performance establishes experience. Investigate theory-specific predictions and ethical uncertainty, not declarations.

The key design principle is *discriminative evidence*: each purportedly mind-like mechanism should make a prediction that a simpler plausible competitor would not make under comparable information and resources.

## Difficulties the program must confront

**The identity problem.** Persistence of facts or stable persona prompts is not the same as historical selfhood. For a causal test, earlier actions, commitments, errors and later corrections must influence choices after explicit resets. A model could reproduce these choices through a sufficient belief-state representation without storing narratives; that is a valid rival, not failure.

**The agency problem.** Background cron jobs can create frequent activity without internally generated aims; conversely, a dormant system may maintain decisions when invoked. We must distinguish action initiation, deliberation and real process uptime from prose about mental activity.

**The development problem.** Apparent learning from retrieval may be cheaper and more stable than continual parameter training. Measure transfer, forgetting, correction provenance, data deletion and distribution shifts before favoring weight-based plasticity.

**The integration problem.** Adding memory, attention, self-model and affects as disconnected modules is insufficient. The same agent must show durable cross-module causal dependencies. Strong controllers with fewer modules must remain eligible.

**The consciousness problem.** Brain findings underdetermine artificial implementations; computation-first and substrate-sensitive theories disagree about relevance. Map discriminable implications honestly. Functional tests may motivate welfare caution without resolving metaphysics.

## DARPA-style eight program questions (adapted)

1. **Objective without jargon:** Build and scientifically evaluate a persistent digital system that learns from experiences and makes coherent decisions over time.
2. **Today:** memory-augmented language models, retrieval, belief-state planners, recurrent systems, model-based agents and social avatars; none establishes an enduring conscious entity by conversational persuasiveness.
3. **New contribution sought:** comparative longitudinal *causal* evidence that tests whether integrated internal organization improves future action over simpler sufficing representations.
4. **Who benefits:** basic AI/cognitive science and, conditional on safety and actual evidence, continuity-aware software such as Anjo.
5. **Risks:** confounding, data leakage, oracle exposure, unmeasured compute advantage, reward hacking, private-data mishandling, ungrounded consciousness marketing and relationship manipulation.
6. **Resources:** limited in-ChatGPT research with user-approved tools; no external paid runner, no authorized autonomous deployment.
7. **Time:** inherently uncertain; scheduled tasks don't guarantee a 40-minute run or independent experiments.
8. **Exams:** G0 well-posed observables, G1 rival predictions, G2 preregistration, G3 reproducible implementation, G4 fair logged results, G5 architecture revision; ultimately E900 staged P0 offline/P1 prospective/P2 actual 90-calendar-day trial, **all E900 stages currently proposed**.

[W2 architecture/plasticity](../workstreams.md) tests whether proposed components and substrates are necessary relative to minimal systems. [W3 consciousness science](../workstreams.md) compares rival theories without confusing indicators with proof.
