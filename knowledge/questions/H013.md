**Question (from v1 E100 Day 6, PR #11).** When the organism reports on itself, is the report grounded in the states
that cause its behaviour, or is it a plausible narrative? v1's liar-twin instrument ran a grounded ledger reader and a
zero-read narrator on the same policy. The grounded reader acted better (3 vs 1), yet a constructed preference metric
favoured the narrator on every probe. A metric that rewards how a report reads cannot tell grounded reports from
confabulated ones.

**v2 operationalisation.** A report is valid to the extent that it predicts the organism's next action better than a
report built without access to its internal state, and to the extent that lesions move report and action together.
The organism already broadcasts its intentions (`pending=` from goals) and its capability forecasts (`self:` from the
self-model). Scoring the self-model's forecasts against outcomes (C017, C018) is a narrow case of this question. Access
lesions (`cut:X>Y`) separate what is reportable (in the workspace) from what reaches the policy. Memory and goals are
degenerate (C004), so a cut to one path may be compensated by the other: cut both when testing for dissociation.

**Strongest rival:** a narrator that sees the same transcript as the organism but none of its internal state. If it
predicts the next action as well as the grounded report, the report adds nothing. Scope: L2, meaning the coupling
between report and cause in this organism. It says nothing about experience ([H006](H006.md)).
