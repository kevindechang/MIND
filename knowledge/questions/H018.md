id: H018
title: Is the missing piece the rule that chooses the plan? Choosing the plan by its posterior expected utility, with unknown nodes integrated out, rather than among fully known plans.
status: evaluated (C096, C098-C101 preregistered; C097 inconclusive on its first held-out evaluation)
next: Preserve the inconclusive C097 boundary; register successors prospectively to isolate planning mediation, test non-myopic planning and remove the supplied DAG hypothesis-space assumption.
---

**Hypothesis.** H015 to H017 established that routed information seeking, with a structure prior learned from experience, pays on the uniform prior. Under censored probes and under the skewed prior the gain was no better than a facts-only organism (C083 refuted; C084, C086 and C093 not confirmed). H018 tests a different component: the rule that chooses the plan. The derived plan recommendation considered only plans whose nodes were all known. Under censoring, a probe that comes back censored leaves the plans through its node unknown, so the rule dropped them. The hypothesis is that the plan rule, not the probing, was the missing piece.

**What was changed.** `causal.posterior_plan=1` chooses the plan with the highest expected utility under the DAG posterior, the same posterior that the value of information uses. Unknown nodes are integrated out. The rule needs at least one known reach fact; with none, it falls back to the policy, as before. Nothing else changes: the probing rule, the learned structure prior (H017), the censor correction, the value carry and the routing bonus are as in H017. The option is off by default, and with it off the code takes the original path. When every node's reach is known, the two rules agree.

**Exploration (dev seeds 0-7, 1500 episodes; not evidence).** Means over eight lifetimes, the same seeds for every arm. Regret is measured against the Bayes-optimal benchmark described under Design.

| variant | plain | compact | known-plan (H017 package) | posterior plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev, utility | -1.678 | -1.674 | -1.401 | **-1.204** |
| dev, regret | 0.560 | 0.556 | 0.283 | **0.086** |
| censored, utility | -1.674 | -1.632 | -1.647 | **-1.494** |
| censored, regret | 0.358 | 0.316 | 0.330 | **0.178** |
| skewed, utility | -1.461 | -1.449 | -1.450 | **-1.210** |
| skewed, regret | 0.335 | 0.323 | 0.325 | **0.084** |

Probes per episode barely change between the known-plan and the posterior rules (dev 0.63 against 0.62, censored 0.52 against 0.53, skewed 0.42 against 0.39), so the gain is in which plan is chosen, not in how much is probed. Success rises from 0.84 to 0.99 on dev, from 0.73 to 0.84 under censoring, and from 0.76 to 0.96 under skew.

**Design choices, fixed before registration.**
- The posterior is the DAG posterior over the 25 labelled graphs, weighted by the learned prior (em=1).
- The comparators: C096 to C098 compare the H017 package with the option off, so the two configurations differ only in the plan rule. C100 and C101 compare the same package with the option on against the plain organism.
- Episodes: 1500 per lifetime, pinned in every claim. Utility and regret are the late window (the second half), with 16 paired lifetimes per claim.
- The benchmark: regret is measured against `oracle_value`, the ex-ante Bayes-optimal value under the true prior and censor model, computed by the legacy E001 v0.4 dynamic program with the permitted history. It does not see the true graph. The `bayes` rival executes the same policy.
- Primary and secondary: C096 to C098 are primary, and they decide the H018 verdict. C099 to C101 are secondary, reported without multiplicity correction.
- Margins: 0.10 utility is 40% of one probe's cost (0.25 per probe, the workshop's cost), and 0.15 regret is 60% of one probe's cost. Both were set before any held-out run.
- The held-out seeds come from the drand beacon after the claim commit is pushed.

**Rival rule and the primary comparison.** All six claims have `about=("causal",)`. For the effect claims (C096 to C098, C100, C101), the standing facts-only rival `compact` (the causal organ's recalled facts without its derived plan) is in the panel, so PREREGISTERED also needs the posterior-plan organism to beat compact with a bootstrap lower bound above zero. A threshold claim has no panel, so C099 is not gated by compact. The gate is separate from the primary comparison. If C096 holds but compact is not beaten, the claim is reported as blocked by the rival, not as a refutation of the plan-rule effect. In H017 the same gate kept C094 and C095 from PREREGISTERED, because facts-only compact matched the learned-prior organism under censoring and skew.

**Regression check.** On the fingerprint check (`mind run` on each arm, 3 seeds, 200 episodes), the registered H015 and H016 arms are byte-identical to their pre-change fingerprints, and the H017 arm is byte-identical to the H017 commit. The change therefore does not move any existing result. It does change the RESULT_CODE fingerprint, so records made on earlier code are refreshed on the current code before any claim above ROBUST is reported.

**Limits, stated before any held-out run.**
- The hypothesis space (25 labelled DAGs with the workshop's reach semantics) and the payoff rule are still supplied. The plan rule does not change that.
- The posterior is exact over the supplied hypotheses, and the learned prior lives over those hypotheses.
- The benchmark knows the true prior and censor model. The organism learns them only in part: the prior by online EM, and the censor rate from its own probes.
- These are performance comparisons. C096 to C098 measure the total effect of the change, including learning and probing feedback. They do not measure mediation by the plan rule, and they do not by themselves establish that the causal organ is integrated in the L2 sense.
- The organism maximises expected utility, so risk attitude is not modelled.
- Three nodes only.
- No multiplicity correction for the secondary claims C099 to C101.

**Strongest rivals.** The known-plan package with the same probing and prior (the primary comparators, C096 to C098); the plain organism (C100, C101); the facts-only compact organism, which every effect claim must also beat through the rival panel. The Bayes-optimal benchmark is a yardstick for C099, not a rival that must be beaten.
