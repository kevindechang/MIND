**Question (opened by C030, C032–C034).** The workspace delivers the same few contents to every organ, and every consumer takes everything it is given. Shadows show what that costs. In vigil, the self-model forecasts better *without* memory's contents. They are informative (real beats a matched sham), but carrying their features dilutes its normalised learning more than they inform it (C030). The policy acts worse when it receives the self-model's contents (C034), and a matched sham of them costs it about two thirds as much (dev exploration), so most of that cost is carrying the features, not their content. Yet in silent outages a single memory content serves both control and metacognition (C033). Global availability helps a consumer that can use a content and hurts one that cannot ignore it.

Global-workspace accounts describe specialised processors that take from the broadcast what they can use. This organism's consumers have no such receiver-side selection; the only selection happens at the workspace (H003). The question is whether receiver-side selection keeps the gains of global availability without its costs, and whether the selection must be learned.

**Strongest rival:** a fixed per-consumer filter chosen with the same information (for example, the best subset of sources for each consumer, picked on dev seeds). If it matches the learned gate, learning adds nothing. A gate that wins only because it was given the answer (e.g. hard-coded to drop memory in vigil) is by construction and must be labelled so.

Scope: L2, this organism and these worlds. Related: [H003](H003.md) (sender-side selection), [H007](H007.md) (integration), [H013](H013.md) (self-reports that reach action).
