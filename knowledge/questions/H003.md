**Hypothesis (v1 H003, sharpened).** Broadcasting a few selected contents to every function improves learning under interference relative to giving every function everything ("flat" direct access) at equal information. Mechanistic reason in this substrate: the policy learns over pairwise conjunctions of what is broadcast, so a narrow workspace trades information for faster credit assignment (the "consciousness prior" idea, Bengio 2017, arXiv:1709.08568 — cited as a lead, not as evidence).

**Conditional prediction tested in v2:** the bottleneck helps when distractors outnumber relevant items (C005, vigil/crowded) and is neutral without them (C006, vigil/dev). Open threads, explored on dev seeds but NOT registered because the evidence did not support them: that the bottleneck hurts after long delays (vigil/long: −0.07 [−0.21, +0.09]), and that disconnecting an organ which still occupies workspace slots is worse than removing it (vigil/dev: +0.02 [−0.06, +0.09]).

**Capacity curve, scoped to this organism.** Dev-seed sweeps of K = 1..8 and unlimited access (exploration, not records) motivated two sealed claims at C005's 0.08 kept-rate bar. C039 tests whether raising distractors from 2 to 5 increases the four-slot advantage over unlimited access by more than 0.08. C040 tests whether lengthening the gap from 3-8 to 9-16 increases the cost of a seven-slot workspace, relative to unlimited access, by more than 0.08. Seven slots is the wide, still-binding end of that curve under two distractors, where late ticks usually offer about seven bids; it is not a claim that every other K does the same. The default four-slot bottleneck on vigil/long was negative in that exploration, but the interval did not clear 0.08, so that hurt was not registered. Outage and workshop showed losses at very small K and no comparable help at four slots. Unregistered noise-by-gap cells are not evidence.

**Rival:** `rival:flat` (identical organism, unlimited workspace). A loss to flat is a real, reportable result.
