**Hypothesis (v1 H010, from R002).** Explicit predictive traces of one's own actions improve causal attribution between self-induced and exogenous changes. Untested. Note the trap v1 hit repeatedly: if the world hands the agent an "external" label, attribution is by construction; the world must make the confound real.

**2026-10-11 H010 world design (DESIGN_ONLY, NOT_EXECUTED in MIND).**
A new shared-substrate world `poltergeist` has hidden, lifetime-stable polarities for two actuator commands. For each episode it pre-draws external shocks and actuator slips independently of *all* agent actions, so paired agents face identical hidden disturbances. Agents see only net dial position and whether their action led to **net** improvement. Private `Step.info` contains the actual decomposition for instrumentation, not for the organism. The world rewards dial regulation with a non-saturating `1/(1+abs(dial))` per tick, and separately reports mean absolute error and objectively wrong attempted corrections (including actuator slips). The held-out variants alter shock and slip rates, not the problem's labels.

**Proposed causal hypothesis (not a sealed claim):** an efference organ predicting the sensory change due to the previous action improves mean reward by more than **1.0 reward point per 24-tick episode** relative to a generic filter with identical observations, previous-action information, outcome feedback and compute budget. One point represents more than four percent of the maximum 24-point episode score, a practically meaningful but deliberately unoptimised target. This is a *prospective design target*, not a margin selected after running data. Both mechanisms must be measured in the same seeded worlds. The strongest rival is an action-conditioned state estimator that subtracts estimated action effects, not the weaker action-blind reactive policy.

**Falsifier:** the paired 95% interval fails to exceed the margin, the equal-information state estimator ties or wins, correction accuracy does not track improvement, or the result relies on action-dependent shocks, privileged labels, or an outcome oracle. A zero-effect or adverse effect must be registered rather than hidden. A no-action policy cannot validate attribution simply by driving `wrong_corrections` to zero; reward is primary and wrong corrections diagnostic.
