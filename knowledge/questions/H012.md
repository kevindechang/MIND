**Hypothesis (v1 H012, from R002).** Learning at several timescales (state, causal memory, procedures, optional weights) improves durable transfer versus any one layer. v2's kernel already supports mid-life lesions, so acute-versus-recovered deficits are measurable.

**What C060 measures.** One vigil life switches variant mid-life: dev, then long, then dev. `@160:freeze:policy` stops policy updates at the first long episode. The plain organism's kept rate shows an acute deficit above 0.10, a recovery above 0.10, and a frozen-policy recovery inside ±0.10. On the return to dev, neither the intact policy nor the frozen one loses more than 0.10 kept relative to its own pre-switch window. `rival:window8` (same TD learner, last eight raw observations, same public stream) is below the organism by more than 0.50 before the switch and does not recover by more than 0.10. Its own acute deficit and immediate return change are estimated and do not decide C060. No new organ: memory and goals reset each episode, and the pre-switch policy weights still carry the original competence, so a second durable timescale is not warranted by this switch.

**Not registered, dev seeds only.** On seeds 0-15, outage dev to silent and back gave an organism acute reward deficit of +1.695 [+0.914, +2.476], above the 0.5 outage bar, but recovery +0.446 [-0.772, +1.665] and the return contrasts straddled ±0.5, including the return versus a life that stayed on dev (+0.092 [-0.431, +0.622] in the last window). Those intervals are exploration, not a record. A within-episode A-B-A world on `contrib/h012-switchback-interference-20261011` is design-only (no claim, no record, stub rival). `contrib/h012-timescales-mac` is not on the remote.

**Scope.** This organism in these worlds. L2 causal organisation, not phenomenal experience. The parent question stays open until a same-cue reversal is measured.
