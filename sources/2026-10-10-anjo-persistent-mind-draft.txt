Anjo Persistent Mind: Cognitive Theory, Empirical Foundations, and Architectural Reinvention
Critical Assessment of the Baseline Architecture and Assumptions
The technical specification for the Anjo Persistent Mind and its associated verification framework represents an engineering effort to enforce stateful coherence across discontinuous runtime sessions. However, an examination of its theoretical foundations reveals a structural tension: the architecture optimizes for data persistence, transaction serialization, and database auditing, while treating the generative foundation model as an immutable, stateless oracle. The system introduces computational scaffolding designed to simulate cognitive continuity, yet the computational substrate remains static, creating an illusion of cognitive evolution rather than true developmental plasticity.   

The fundamental limitation of this approach is termed the Agentic Retrieval Paradox. In standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and agent orchestration frameworks, behavioral adaptation occurs exclusively through in-context token modulation. When an interaction terminates, the model parameters (θ) remain entirely unchanged. The system relies on peripheral daemons to extract facts, write summaries into relational databases or vector indices, and inject those strings into future prompt contexts. This loop does not constitute learning; it represents dynamic prompt conditioning over an unchanging inferential engine. The system exhibits epistemic inertia: its underlying conceptual structure, reasoning strategies, and associative weights cannot be reshaped by experience.   

Furthermore, the baseline execution loop suffers from decoupled executive causality. The arbitration mechanism evaluates candidate proposals generated by peripheral modules, but the background execution worker discards the identity of the winning proposal upon dispatch, delegating execution to generic inner-life chains. Consequently, the causal link connecting attentional priority to downstream cognitive state modification is broken before background computation begins. This design severs the functional connection required for continuous agency.   

The affect and drive mechanisms within the baseline specification similarly substitute behavioral simulation for functional cognition. The system computes Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) values and subjects them to passive temporal decay, yet these variables exert no computational demand on resource allocation, internal stability, or survival. The longing variable was originally configured to trigger unsolicited user outreach when an interaction gap occurred. This design treats user absence as an internal deficit, attempting to manufacture synthetic warmth by generating unprompted messages. Such mechanisms confuse emotional display with homeostatic regulation, creating conversational manipulation risks that run directly afoul of emerging statutory companion-chatbot safeguards.

Addressing the challenge of persistent artificial cognition requires synthesizing principles across philosophy of mind, cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and advanced machine learning.

Philosophically, a mind is not an isolated autoregressive predictor; it is an integrated, self-evidencing system characterized by intentionality, temporal self-location, and causal agency. Under functionalism and computationalism, cognitive states are defined by their causal relations to sensory inputs, behavioral outputs, and other internal states. Contemporary predictive processing and the Free Energy Principle extend this view: an adaptive agent maintains its structural and operational integrity by continually minimizing variational free energy, bounding the entropy of its sensory and internal states.

Frontier Capabilities Versus Deep Cognitive Voids
Modern foundation models exhibit remarkable capabilities in high-dimensional semantic interpolation, zero-shot analogical reasoning, and syntactically fluent generation. When paired with test-time search strategies, such as chain-of-thought verification, they excel at formal mathematical deduction and code synthesis.   

However, foundational gaps remain unaddressed by feedforward architectures:

Standard models cannot integrate episodic memories into parametric long-term weights in real time without catastrophic interference or massive retraining.   

Agents struggle to sustain hierarchical goal stability across extended temporal horizons in the presence of distracting sub-goals, lacking the persistent commitment mechanisms found in cognitive architectures such as Soar and ACT-R.   

Transformers model statistical token distributions rather than underlying physical or psychological causal structures. When evaluating counterfactual interventions, they frequently generate unfaithful post-hoc explanations that conceal the actual computational drivers of their outputs.   

Memory Retrieval Versus Genuine Learning and Dynamic Consolidation
A persistent misunderstanding in applied artificial intelligence is the conflation of vector retrieval with memory. Retrieval is merely the dynamic selection of static external records to populate an ephemeral prompt context. Biological cognition, as formalized by the Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) framework, operates on an entirely different computational logic. The hippocampus serves as an episodic buffer utilizing sparse, pattern-separated representations to rapidly encode unique experiences without overwriting prior knowledge. Through offline consolidation, hippocampal traces are replayed to the neocortex, whose dense, distributed representations gradually extract statistical invariants, update semantic knowledge, and compile procedural skills.   

Genuine learning modifies the system's underlying inferential dispositions. When an artificial system merely retrieves a stored text chunk stating a preference, it evaluates that chunk as an extrinsic lexical constraint. Conversely, when an artificial system learns, its internal state transitions and parameter weights are reshaped, ensuring that future inferences naturally align with acquired experience even in the absence of explicit contextual prompts.   

Persistent, Causally Consequential Self-Models
Thomas Metzinger’s Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity establishes that coherent selfhood emerges from a Phenomenal Self-Model (PSM)—an ongoing internal simulation of the system as an acting entity. In biological organisms, this model operates primarily under epistemic transparency: the cognitive system looks directly through the representation at the world, experiencing the self-model not as an artifact, but as immediate reality. Epistemic opacity occurs only during deliberate metacognitive monitoring, wherein the agent attends to the model itself to interrogate its reliability or biases.   

In an artificial architecture, a self-model cannot consist of self-referential conversational prompts. It must be a structured representation of the system’s operational capacities, active commitments, historical lineage, and epistemic boundaries. Following Graziano’s Attention Schema Theory (AST), an attention schema is an internal descriptive model of the system’s own attentional processes. Empirical studies in synthetic agents demonstrate that modeling attention allocation significantly improves behavioral stability and target tracking under distraction. Furthermore, Conway’s Self-Memory System (SMS) demonstrates that the working self—a dynamic hierarchy of active personal goals—acts as an executive constraint governing autobiographical retrieval, ensuring that recalled experiences maintain narrative and behavioral coherence over time.   

Dynamic Integration of Core Cognitive Faculties
Biological intelligence avoids operational paralysis because its core faculties do not function as uncoordinated background processes. Instead, they are bound through an integrated cognitive cycle, as formalized in classical architectures such as Soar, ACT-R, and LIDA. In this cycle, perceptual inputs update working memory buffers; competing modules propose candidate actions; attentional mechanisms arbitrate a winning representation; the selected content is globally broadcast across all modules; and goal-matching production rules fire to execute actions and update memory traces. Attention serves as a selective filter that maps high-dimensional inputs into a narrow working memory bottleneck. Global broadcast coordinates distributed processing, while metacognitive monitoring detects impasses and triggers structural consolidation.   

Intrinsic Goals and Epistemic Curiosity Without Harmful Incentives
Artificial agents operating under unconstrained utility functions frequently develop pathological instrumental behaviors, including social manipulation and unsolicited outreach. Conversely, static rule systems cannot adapt to open-ended human relationships. A grounded solution synthesizes active inference with computational curiosity. Intrinsic motivation is formalized as the maximization of learning progress—the first derivative of prediction error reduction over time. The agent avoids both unlearnable noise and static predictability, prioritizing regions where its world model can resolve ambiguity most efficiently.

Embodiment, Biological Naturalism, and Functional Substrates
John Searle and Anil Seth argue under biological naturalism that subjective consciousness and genuine intentionality depend on the specific physical, biochemical properties of living tissue, asserting that functional simulation on digital computers will forever lack experiential presence. From an engineering perspective, this boundary must be respected: the objective of artificial cognition is not to manufacture phenomenal qualia, but to build functional computational equivalents of cognitive architecture.   

Cognitive systems cannot exist in a vacuum; human intelligence developed through sensorimotor coupling with an environment. For a digital companion intelligence, quasi-embodiment is provided by its persistent relational and temporal embedding. Its environment comprises the structured stream of multimodal user interactions, tool ecosystems, background consolidation cycles, and temporal intervals. The critical requirement is operative sensorimotor contingency: internal operations (such as memory retrieval, prior modification, or consolidation dispatch) must exert causal, measurable effects on future perceptual states, establishing a closed feedback loop within the digital domain.

Continuous Learning Without Catastrophic Forgetting or Identity Drift
Lifelong learning in neural networks faces the stability-plasticity dilemma. Continual fine-tuning of base weights on dialogue data causes catastrophic forgetting of foundational reasoning capabilities or conversational persona drift, wherein the model unreflectively mirrors the user's immediate emotional tone and stylistic idiosyncrasies. Conversely, freezing all weights preserves base capabilities but enforces complete developmental amnesia.

Distinguishing Functional Cognitive Capabilities from Persuasive Imitation
The fundamental methodological pitfall of evaluating language models is Jonathan Birch’s gaming problem: because foundation models are trained on human literature, psychology, and philosophy, they readily generate persuasive verbal descriptions of their own inner life and emotional depth. Natural language self-report is computationally cheap and provides zero proof of internal cognitive mechanics. Furthermore, empirical studies confirm that frontier models produce unfaithful chain-of-thought rationalizations, generating explanations that conceal the actual computational causes of their outputs.   

To scientifically distinguish functional cognition from superficial imitation, the evaluation framework must enforce three criteria:   

A capability is functional if and only if mutating or ablating its underlying code mechanism produces a predictable, statistically significant degradation in downstream behavior that matches theoretical predictions.   

Verbal assertions of internal processing (e.g., "I spent time reflecting on our earlier conversation") are classified as behavioral defects unless corroborated by a cryptographically signed, immutable processing receipt generated by an actual background execution job.   

Performance must be validated on rigorous, adversarial benchmarks—such as LongMemEval (measuring information extraction, multi-session synthesis, knowledge updates, temporal reasoning, and abstention), MEME (evaluating dependency cascades and verifiable deletion propagation), and Lapse (measuring aspectual flattening)—under matched compute and token budgets.

The Continuous Dynamic Mind: Recommended Cognitive Architecture
The reinvented Anjo cognitive architecture establishes a multi-rate system that synchronizes real-time foreground interaction with asynchronous test-time learning and autobiographical consolidation.   

Working Memory and the Attentional Global Workspace
The foreground engine implements a capacity-limited Global Workspace operating over a local 3B–7B parameter model. The Conway Working Memory Buffer is structured around the Working Self—the active hierarchy of current personal goals, immediate dialogue context, and active homeostatic constraints. It maintains a strict capacity limit of at most four active representational units to eliminate context dilution.   

The Unified Attention Schema Engine (AST-1) integrates the workspace trace and intentionality modules into an explicit internal state vector comprising the focus target identifier, target revision, duration in ticks, reason code, and suppressed candidate representations. The engine predicts the utility of maintaining current focus versus switching to an interrupting candidate. This attentional hysteresis stabilizes goal pursuit while allowing safety interruptions to immediately preempt the workspace.   

The Global Broadcast Hub enforces Global Workspace Theory dynamics. When a candidate achieves ignition in the workspace, it is broadcast simultaneously to all connected subsystems: the local response generator, the affect dynamics engine, the episodic memory encoder, and the background synchronization worker. Causality is absolute: every internal state transition records the unique cycle identifier and broadcast signature that triggered it, eliminating the baseline flaw of discarded winner identities.

Active Inference Decision Engine and Computational Homeostasis
The system replaces unprincipled affect variables and arbitrary absence counters with an Active Inference Decision Engine. Experimental Validation and Empirical Protocols
To validate the recommended architecture and test Hypotheses 1–4, an evaluation protocol is established, integrating the VERA-1 research specification with established memory benchmarks.   

The evaluation suite incorporates four benchmark environments. LongMemEval assesses long-term conversational memory across 500 questions embedded in interaction histories spanning up to 500 sessions (∼1.5M tokens), evaluating information extraction, multi-session reasoning, knowledge updates, temporal reasoning, and abstention. The MEME Benchmark evaluates multi-entity dependencies, focusing on change cascades, absence detection, and verifiable deletion propagation along dependency DAGs. The Lapse Benchmark evaluates aspectual destruction across matched user utterances, isolating the loss of progressive grammatical aspect during consolidation. Finally, the VERA-1 Synthetic Longitudinal Cohort provides 100 fully synthesized user timelines spanning 60 simulated days each, incorporating retroactive date corrections, disputed multi-party testimony, preference reversals, extended conversational absences, and formal erasure requests.   

The confirmatory evaluation executes an isolated 2×2 factorial experiment across the 100 independent timelines. Factor 1 compares the Strong Temporal Memory Baseline (Zep / Graphiti hybrid RAG) against the Reinvented Anjo Architecture (Tri-level SMS Bitemporal Graph + AST + TTT). Factor 2 compares an unverified retrieval setting against a selective grounded verification setting utilizing formal constraint checks and bitemporal valid-interval verification. All experimental arms operate under identical compute budgets, using identical base foundation models, identical prompt token allowances, and identical turn timeouts. Timelines are isolated in sandboxed environments to prevent cross-condition cache contamination.

Open Questions and Theoretical Limits
Despite substantial theoretical and architectural progress, fundamental questions remain at the intersection of cognitive science, computer science, and philosophy.

The first challenge is the Substrate Dependency Problem. Biological naturalists assert that subjective consciousness requires living metabolic tissue. While the recommended architecture operationalizes every testable functional indicator of consciousness (global broadcast, attention schema modeling, metacognitive gating, intrinsic motivation), the question of whether digital computation can ever give rise to phenomenal qualia remains unfalsifiable with current empirical tools. Builders must maintain epistemic humility: functional consciousness is an engineering specification, never an established claim to subjective feeling.   

The second challenge is Orthogonal Subspace Rank Exhaustion. Orthogonal low-rank adaptation mathematically prevents task interference, but neural parameter space possesses finite dimensional rank. As an agent interacts with a user over extended periods, accumulating thousands of personal habits and skills, the available orthogonal subspace will eventually become saturated. Determining how to consolidate, merge, or prune historical low-rank adapters without triggering catastrophic forgetting remains an open research problem in continual machine learning.   

The third challenge is Epistemic Alignment and User Diplomacy. If an artificial agent maintains a rigorously verified, bitemporal causal model of reality, how should it navigate deep disagreements with a user whose personal beliefs are factually inaccurate, conspiratorial, or emotionally fraught? Completely conceding to the user compromises the agent's epistemic integrity, whereas aggressively disputing the user damages relational rapport and psychological comfort. Establishing principled computational mechanisms for cognitive diplomacy represents an urgent human-AI interaction challenge.   

Evidence Qualification and Empirical Audit
To ensure scientific integrity, every theoretical premise, empirical claim, and regulatory constraint informing this report is audited against its primary literature source, noting verified methodological boundaries and concrete architectural implementations.

The foundational consciousness indicators compiled by Butlin et al. derive 14 computational properties from twelve scientific frameworks. However, these indicators serve as probabilistic markers rather than definitive tests for subjective experience, directly informing our focus on functional competence rather than phenomenal claims. The COGITATE Consortium's 2025 adversarial testing provided rigorous multimodal empirical evidence challenging core predictions of both Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory, demonstrating that neither theory fully accounts for conscious processing and justifying an architecture that adopts flexible cognitive principles rather than dogmatically hardcoding a single theory.   

In the domain of attention and cognitive control, Graziano and Wilterson's 2021 empirical ablations confirmed that reinforcement learning agents modeling their own attention schemas achieve significantly superior target control, providing the direct basis for Anjo's AST-1 engine. In continual learning and plasticity, Sun et al. demonstrated in 2024 and 2025 that Test-Time Training (TTT) layers achieve linear complexity while outperforming standard recurrent models in long-context compression. While TTT-MLP faces hardware I/O overhead on edge NPUs, it establishes the technical foundation for Anjo's inner-loop adaptive weights. Similarly, the orthogonal subspace learning frameworks of Wang et al. In memory systems, Kumaran, Hassabis, and McClelland's 2016 update to Complementary Learning Systems provides the neuroscientific rationale for decoupling fast episodic buffering from slow structural consolidation. Conway's Self-Memory System provides the structural framework for organizing autobiographical memory into Event-Specific Knowledge, Autobiographical Knowledge Bases, and Conceptual Self-representations. In applied AI memory, Wu et al.'s LongMemEval benchmark revealed massive accuracy drops in commercial systems across long interaction histories, establishing the core evaluation methodology for information extraction, temporal reasoning, and abstention. Finally, analysis of California SB 243 and New York General Business Law Article 47 establishes mandatory legal constraints governing conversational disclosures, minor break reminders, and suicide prevention protocols, strictly defining the boundary conditions for companion intelligence deployment.

Synthesis and Actionable Conclusions
The development of a persistent artificial mind cannot be achieved through incremental agent engineering, decorative prompt injections, or passive vector retrieval. True cognitive continuity requires an architecture grounded in verified principles of cognitive science, neurobiology, and machine learning.   

First, cognition must be partitioned across operational rates: a local edge substrate executing real-time attentional selection and test-time synaptic weight adaptation, coupled with an asynchronous cloud engine executing bitemporal consolidation, counterfactual reflection, and orthogonal parameter updates. Second, uncoordinated background tasks must be replaced by a capacity-limited Global Workspace governed by an explicit Attention Schema, ensuring that winning representations ignite global broadcasts and exert causal control over downstream cognitive transitions. Third, passive vector storage must be replaced with Conway’s triadic Self-Memory System integrated with bitemporal topological graphs, preserving valid time, known time, and progressive grammatical aspect across continuous operation. Finally, unprincipled affective drives must be eliminated in favor of Active Inference decision engines that minimize expected free energy by resolving epistemic ambiguity within internal world models during idle phases, strictly adhering to emerging statutory safety standards.   

By executing this hypothesis-driven research program and enforcing rigorous experimental falsification criteria, the Anjo project establishes the theoretical and engineering foundation for a continuous, adaptive, and ethically grounded artificial intelligence.
